## 1. The Problem You Already Have

If you build, sell, or deploy anything in vehicle-to-grid, vehicle-to-home, or bidirectional charging, you already live inside one gap:

**Every specification sheet says "bidirectional capable." Almost nothing is actually ready to export — here, today, lawfully, safely, and under settled economics.**

You explain this gap in every sales meeting, every pilot post-mortem, every frustrated customer call — in long sentences, because the industry has no word for it. V2G, V2H, V2X, ISO 15118, OCPP: all of them describe *directions, interfaces, and protocols*. None of them answers the only question your customer actually asks: **"Is my flow ready?"**

The distance between declared capability and governed, running flow is where V2G pilots stall, where sales over-promise, and where deployment budgets disappear. That distance had no name and no classification.

Now it does.

## 2. What This Asset Is

**Outrush Readiness** is the actual-readiness layer for reverse energy flow from electric vehicles — the measured distance between declared capability and a flow that is permitted, supported, safe, directed, operated, and economically governed.

> **V2X tells you what is possible. Outrush Readiness tells you what is ready.**

The asset is not a domain with a landing page. It is a complete, governed category standard:

- **The O-Scale (O0–O5):** six defined states from *Declared Capability* to *Governed Flow*, each with an explicit boundary condition and an explicit non-implication. A configuration holds the highest level whose conditions it fully satisfies.
- **The Six Readiness Dimensions:** Permission, Equipment, Battery, Destination, Operation, Economics — the examination headings behind every classification.
- **A conformant classification statement format** — usable in a customer report on day one.
- **A governance record** (OUTRUSH_READINESS_STANDARD.md) with versioned change control and a git-verifiable priority record of the category definition.
- **A complete visual identity** — the O-Scale orbital diagram, typography system, and reference-environment design language, live at EVOutrush.com.

The standard is deliberately **specification-only** in its current form: it classifies states; it does not score, certify, audit, or rank, and it names no vendors. Its record is clean.

## 3. Why Owning the Standard Beats Building One

The vocabulary of a category is infrastructure. Whoever holds the neutral classification language holds three operating tools their competitors do not:

**As a sales instrument.** "We take your fleet from O0 to O5" is a proposition your competitors — who sell hardware or software features — cannot phrase. The scale turns your delivery process into a named, ordered journey the customer can see.

**As a qualification instrument.** Classifying a prospect's configuration on the O-Scale in the first conversation separates deployable customers from aspirational ones before you spend engineering time. The Six Dimensions are a due-diligence checklist that already exists in your sales engineers' heads — now with shared language.

**As a positioning instrument.** Competitors sell chargers, platforms, or consulting hours. The owner of the readiness standard sells the definition of *done*. In an early category, the party that names the states of the market tends to be the reference the market argues inside of.

Building an equivalent internally costs more than acquiring this one — and an internal framework carries your brand's bias by birth. This one was born neutral, and its git history proves the priority date.

## 4. What You Do With It on Day One — Three Scenarios

**If you make bidirectional chargers / EVSE:** Position every product line against the scale ("hardware that closes O1"). Publish the standard as your market-education layer — every buyer who learns the O-Scale learns why your equipment matters, without you naming a competitor.

**If you run a V2G / fleet-energy platform:** Make the O-Scale your onboarding diagnostic. Every fleet enters as a classified configuration; your platform's value is the movement O2 → O5. Your customer reports gain a vocabulary their CFOs understand in one reading.

**If you advise on fleet electrification / energy strategy:** The standard becomes your methodology's public face. The classification statement format in §6 of the standard is a deliverable template. You inherit a citable reference — with a live site — instead of a slide deck only your firm has seen.
